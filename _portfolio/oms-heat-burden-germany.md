This research output provides a reference implementation of the **Observation-based Mean-Shift (OMS)** framework for quantifying how mean-state warming changes extreme-summer heat burden in Germany.

OMS preserves observed day-to-day weather variability while shifting only the seasonal mean state. This makes it possible to construct counterfactual summer climates corresponding to pre-industrial, present-day, +1.5 °C, and +2.0 °C global-warming levels while retaining the observed intra-seasonal variability.

**Study region:** Germany  
**Study period:** 2004–2024  
**Extreme summers analysed:** 2018, 2019, and 2022  
**Method:** Observation-based Mean-Shift (OMS)  
**Key metric:** Extreme Heat Burden (EHD), expressed in °C day  
**Applications:** Heat-risk assessment, climate attribution, adaptation planning, and subnational exposure analysis  
**Repository:** [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/21005081)

The implementation also supports subnational analyses across German federal states, including per-capita heat burden and distributional differences in exposure.
